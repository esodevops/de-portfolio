Project overview

From storefront pages to a trusted data asset.

The source website is designed for scraping practice but its catalogue still spans pagination, product cards, and individual detail pages. PagePulse automates that journey, standardizes each record, and creates a reusable foundation for pricing, assortment, rating, and availability analysis.

The challenge

Web pages are not analytics tables.

Useful catalogue reporting requires more than downloading HTML. The pipeline has to navigate the site, resolve product links, extract consistent fields, enforce useful types, and preserve a repeatable path into the database.

01

Paginated catalogue

Product records are distributed across 50 pages rather than exposed as one downloadable dataset.

02

Nested detail data

Category information lives on individual book pages and requires a second navigation step.

03

Presentation-first values

Prices contain currency symbols, ratings arrive as words, and stock text includes visual whitespace.

04

Relative asset links

Product and cover-image paths must be resolved into complete URLs for downstream reuse.

05

Data quality

Types, required values, rating ranges, prices, and unique product URLs need explicit validation.

06

Business usability

Raw records become more valuable when paired with readable queries tied to real decisions.

Data pipeline

Extract. Transform. Load. Analyze.

A transparent notebook workflow takes the project from a browser session to indexed PostgreSQL data and documented analysis.

01

Discover

Confirm source availability and establish the catalogue URL pattern.

02

Extract

Use Selenium to visit catalogue and product pages, then parse HTML with Beautiful Soup.

03

Structure

Capture titles, prices, availability, ratings, categories, product URLs, and image URLs.

04

Transform

Convert prices to numeric values and map written ratings to a consistent one-to-five scale.

05

Persist

Write raw and cleaned CSV layers so the extraction output remains inspectable and reusable.

06

Load

Create the PagePulse PostgreSQL schema and load clean records through SQLAlchemy or psql.

07

Analyze

Run plain-language SQL for catalogue, category, pricing, rating, value, and stock insights.

Business analytics

Queries written for decisions.

Instead of abstract SQL exercises, each analysis starts with a clear commercial question and returns an output that a catalogue or merchandising team can understand.

01

Catalogue health

Measure assortment size, category coverage, average price, average rating, and stock percentage.

02

Category performance

Compare category depth, price position, average rating, and highly rated inventory.

03

Premium opportunities

Identify in-stock books that combine above-average pricing with strong ratings.

04

Customer value

Surface affordable books with four- or five-star ratings for value-led promotions.

05

Rating mix

Understand how much of the assortment falls within each customer rating level.

06

Price architecture

Group books into budget, mid-range, and premium bands and compare their ratings.

07

Assortment improvement

Find sizeable categories with low average ratings and a concentration of weak products.

08

Availability risk

Highlight categories with the greatest share of products currently unavailable.

09

Stock turnover

Estimate how quickly books move based on availability patterns and rating velocity.

10

Category gaps

Identify underrepresented categories with high demand potential based on rating distribution.
